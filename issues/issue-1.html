Weekly Intelligence Briefing

AI in Financial Services

Week of May 7 – 13, 2026  |  Issue #1

Created by Carl Jakobsson

The Week at a Glance

A defining week for AI in financial services. Anthropic launched 10 purpose-built AI agents for Wall Street at a marquee New York briefing, while forming a $1.5B joint venture with Goldman Sachs, Blackstone, and others. The IMF sounded the alarm on AI-driven cyber risk to financial stability. The Bank of England's PRA flagged advanced AI models as a source of "significant disruption." OpenAI responded with Daybreak, its own cybersecurity platform. And Cambridge's landmark 2026 report confirmed the industry's AI adoption has hit 81% — but flagged a widening gap between deployment and measurable business transformation.

01

Deals, Funding & M&A

Anthropic, Goldman & Blackstone Launch $1.5B AI Services Venture

Anthropic announced a joint venture with Goldman Sachs, Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, Apollo, and General Atlantic to deploy Claude directly inside businesses. The $1.5B entity will start with portfolio companies owned by the investment partners — targeting mid-sized firms across healthcare, manufacturing, and financial services that lack in-house AI expertise. Goldman's Marc Nachmann described it as tackling a "big shortage of people who know how to apply these tools into businesses."

So What: This isn't a software licence deal — it's an operating model. Anthropic is embedding engineers inside PE-backed companies, effectively becoming the AI deployment layer for the private capital ecosystem. If you're a mid-market FS firm owned by one of these sponsors, expect a call. If you compete with one, this raises the bar on what "AI-enabled operations" means.

Q1 2026 AI Funding Eclipses All of 2025

PitchBook data shows that AI funding in Q1 2026 has already surpassed the full-year 2025 total. Of 1,546 deals recorded, just three — OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI — accounted for 67% of capital. Sovereign wealth funds and corporates provided much of the financing. Meanwhile, global venture funding to fintech startups hit $12B across 751 deals through early April, with vertical AI applied to regulated finance workflows emerging as the dominant theme.

So What: The concentration of capital in three frontier labs underscores how bifurcated AI investment has become. For FS firms evaluating AI partners, the message is clear: the infrastructure layer is consolidating fast. Vertical AI in regulated finance — tax, insurance underwriting, fraud, KYC — is where the actionable opportunity sits for most firms.

02

New Products & Use Cases

Anthropic Unveils 10 Pre-Built AI Agents for Financial Services

At an invite-only briefing in New York — attended by JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon — Anthropic released 10 ready-to-run AI agent templates targeting banking, insurance, asset management, and fintech. The agents can draft pitch decks, review financial statements, screen KYC files, close monthly books, and escalate compliance cases. They're deployable as plugins via Claude Cowork or Claude Code, integrating with existing tools like Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook. FactSet shares fell 8.1% on the announcement; Morningstar, S&P Global, and Moody's all saw sharp selling.

So What: The market's reaction tells the story. When a frontier lab ships agents that do the work of research analysts and compliance associates — and data terminal stocks drop the same day — it's a signal that AI is moving from "copilot" to "replacement for workflow." Middle-office and ops leaders should be running pilots with these agents now, not in Q4.

OpenAI Launches Daybreak — AI-Powered Cybersecurity Platform

OpenAI launched Daybreak on May 11, a cybersecurity initiative combining GPT-5.5 models with its Codex Security agent. The platform scans codebases, identifies attack paths, validates vulnerabilities, and proposes patches — reducing analysis that previously took hours to minutes. Cloudflare, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Oracle, and Zscaler are early partners. The launch is a direct competitive response to Anthropic's Project Glasswing and Claude Mythos, which helped Mozilla discover and patch over 270 Firefox vulnerabilities in April.

So What: Both Anthropic and OpenAI are now positioning their frontier models as core cybersecurity infrastructure. For CISOs and technology risk leaders in FS, this is a strategic procurement decision, not just a tools evaluation. The models that find your vulnerabilities could also be the models that adversaries use to find them. The "defender's advantage" window is narrowing.

03

Use Cases in Action

InsurTech Goes All-In: AI Captures 95% of Insurance Funding in Q1

Gallagher Re's Q1 2026 Global InsurTech Report reveals that AI-labelled insurtechs captured a record 95.2% of all sector funding — $1.55B across 68 deals out of $1.63B total. In a single week in early May, $820M was deployed across four transactions, each AI-native and each targeting a different layer of the insurance stack: Reserv ($125M Series C for AI-native claims administration), Counterpart ($50M Series C for agentic underwriting), and Corgi ($160M Series B). Underwriting timelines are collapsing from three days to three minutes, with straight-through processing rates jumping from 10–15% to 70–90%.

So What: Insurance is no longer "experimenting with AI" — it is funding the companies that will replace current operating models. If you're an incumbent insurer or a broker still running manual submission intake and multi-day underwriting cycles, the competitive threat isn't theoretical. The underwriter's role is shifting from "read the 14-page submission" to "approve or escalate the AI's recommendation."

Duck Creek Launches Agentic AI Platform for Underwriting & Claims

Duck Creek Technologies introduced an insurance-focused Agentic AI Platform with two initial applications: an Agentic Underwriting Workbench and an Agentic First Notice of Loss (FNOL) tool. The platform enables multi-agent systems that autonomously manage end-to-end workflows — from submission intake and data enrichment through to risk scoring and claims triage — escalating to human underwriters and adjusters only when needed. The launch targets P&C carriers looking to move past pilot-stage AI into production deployment.

So What: When a core insurance technology vendor — not a startup — ships agentic AI for underwriting and FNOL, it signals that autonomous workflows are ready for mainstream carrier adoption. This lowers the barrier for mid-market insurers who lack the engineering resources to build their own. Operations and claims leaders should evaluate how this fits their existing Duck Creek stack.

Agentic AI in KYC: Banks Deploy Source-of-Wealth Agents at Scale

Multiple global banks are now deploying agentic AI across the full KYC lifecycle, with source-of-wealth verification emerging as a breakthrough use case. AI agents extract information from client documents, identify missing data, generate source-of-wealth narratives, and review those narratives for accuracy and completeness — with human analysts retaining final sign-off. Accenture reports that one European insurer is applying the same multi-agent architecture to property, casualty, and motor claims, where incorrect decisions carry significant economic and customer impact.

So What: Source-of-wealth has been one of the most labour-intensive and error-prone steps in onboarding high-net-worth clients. If agents can reliably draft and self-check SoW narratives, it unlocks capacity across compliance teams without compromising the human-in-the-loop oversight that regulators expect. Compliance heads should be benchmarking these implementations against their current cost-per-case metrics.

JPMorgan's Proxy IQ: AI Replaces Third-Party Proxy Advisers Entirely

JPMorgan Asset & Wealth Management became the first major investment manager to fully eliminate reliance on external proxy advisory firms for US shareholder voting. The bank replaced ISS and Glass Lewis with Proxy IQ, an AI-powered platform built on its Spectrum data infrastructure. Proxy IQ aggregates and analyses proprietary data from over 3,000 annual company meetings, handling everything from data collection to voting recommendations. The move comes amid increased regulatory scrutiny of the proxy advisory industry's market dominance.

So What: This is one of the clearest examples of AI eliminating an entire category of third-party vendor — not just automating a task, but replacing a $2B+ advisory industry. Asset managers running their own stewardship teams should evaluate whether similar in-house AI tools could reduce external dependency and cost. Proxy advisory firms themselves face an existential strategic question.

Hedge Funds' Record AI-Driven Pivot Into Asian Equities

Morgan Stanley's prime brokerage reported that the week ending May 7 marked the most aggressive period of hedge fund buying in Asian equities since 2010. Goldman Sachs separately noted that April was the largest month for hedge fund inflows into Asian stocks in a decade. The capital flowed overwhelmingly into semiconductor and AI hardware stocks — TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix — as hedge funds chase exposure to Asia's chip supply chain. Multi-strategy platforms are deploying AI-driven quantitative models alongside fundamental strategies to navigate the volatility.

So What: AI is both the trade and the trading tool. Hedge funds are using AI-driven quantitative models to allocate capital into the companies building AI infrastructure. For capital markets desks and prime brokerage teams, the concentration of flow into Asian tech creates both opportunity and crowding risk. Portfolio managers should watch for position-herding dynamics if sentiment shifts.

Agentic Commerce & AI Payments: Stripe Launches Streaming Stablecoin Payments

Stripe introduced "streaming payments" — an AI-native business model combining precise usage tracking with stablecoin micropayments — enabling businesses to get paid for every token the moment it's used. The company also launched Stripe Treasury, a global business account in 15 currencies, and digital asset accounts built with Privy to let developers build fintech apps on stablecoins via a single API. Ramp, Deel, and DoorDash are already building on it. Meanwhile, Visa's stablecoin settlement run rate hit $4.5B annualised — up 460% year-on-year.

So What: "Agentic commerce" — where AI agents execute payments autonomously — is no longer theoretical. When Stripe builds stablecoin micropayment rails specifically for AI-to-AI transactions, it signals that traditional payment infrastructure is being rebuilt around machines, not humans. Payments, treasury, and digital banking teams need to evaluate whether their current rails can support real-time, AI-initiated settlement.

04

Regulation & Compliance

Bank of England's PRA Flags Advanced AI Models as Source of "Significant Disruption"

PRA chief Sam Woods warned this week that it was "reasonable to expect quite significant disruption" from advanced AI models, specifically citing Anthropic's Claude Mythos and OpenAI's ChatGPT 5.5 Instant. He highlighted that these models are increasingly capable of identifying security weaknesses in banking systems, and that banks must patch vulnerabilities faster as unresolved technical flaws remain one of the biggest causes of system outages.

So What: When the PRA names specific AI models as systemic risks, the compliance implications are immediate. Expect supervisory letters and onsite exam questions about your firm's AI threat assessment, patching cadence, and third-party model dependency mapping. This is no longer a forward-looking horizon scan — it's today's exam agenda.

IMF Warns AI-Fuelled Cyberattacks Pose Macro-Financial Risk

The International Monetary Fund published a blog on May 7 warning that advanced AI models can "dramatically reduce the time and cost needed to identify and exploit vulnerabilities," potentially creating correlated failures across the financial system. The Fund noted that reliance on a small number of software platforms, cloud providers, and AI models concentrates risk, and that AI-assisted attacks could propagate across finance, energy, and telecoms simultaneously. The IMF called for cyber stress testing, scenario analysis, and stronger international coordination.

So What: The IMF is explicitly linking AI to systemic financial stability risk — not hypothetically, but by naming specific models and scenarios. For risk and resilience teams, this elevates AI-driven cyber scenarios from tabletop exercises to board-level reporting. Firms using a single cloud or AI provider should revisit concentration risk frameworks.

EU AI Act High-Risk Deadline Approaches: August 2, 2026

With fewer than three months to go, financial services firms face the EU AI Act's August 2, 2026 deadline for high-risk AI system compliance. Credit scoring, fraud detection, and automated decision-making systems must meet transparency, traceability, human oversight, and risk management requirements. The Colorado AI Act also takes effect on June 30, 2026, imposing disclosure, consumer notification, and impact assessment obligations on high-risk AI systems affecting financial services.

So What: Two major compliance deadlines hit within five weeks of each other. Firms running AI in credit, fraud, or automated decisioning across EU or Colorado jurisdictions need to confirm right now that documentation, impact assessments, and human-in-the-loop protocols are audit-ready. The implementation window is effectively closed — this is remediation time.

FCA Opens Supercharged Sandbox Cohort 2 & Expands AI Live Testing

The FCA opened applications for Cohort 2 of its NVIDIA-powered Supercharged Sandbox on May 5, with a deadline of June 1. This round specifically targets agentic AI — including agentic payment services, compliance agents, and customer service agents. Separately, the second AI Live Testing cohort began in April, with eight firms selected including Barclays, Experian, Lloyds Banking Group (Scottish Widows), UBS, and GoCardless. Lloyds' Scottish Widows arm is testing AI-enabled targeted investment support to address the UK's advice gap. The FCA will publish a "good and poor practice" report for AI in financial services later this year, with a full evaluation due Q1 2027.

So What: The FCA's sandbox is now the largest supervised AI-in-financial-services experiment by any Western regulator. For UK-regulated firms — especially neobanks, digital banks, and fintechs building AI-native products — applying for Cohort 2 before June 1 is a strategic priority. Sandbox participation provides regulatory air cover, and the upcoming "good and poor practice" report will effectively set the standard for AI deployment across UK financial services.

05

New Partnerships

Customers Bank Signs Multiyear OpenAI Partnership with Embedded Engineers

Customers Bank, a smaller lender focused on the startup and VC community, signed a multiyear deal with OpenAI that will embed AI engineers directly inside the bank. The partnership targets automating lending and client onboarding — with CEO Sam Sidhu projecting that the bank's efficiency ratio will improve from roughly 49% to the low 40s. The arrangement is symbiotic: OpenAI is co-developing finance tools it could eventually sell to other banks. In an unconventional twist, Sidhu revealed on an earnings call that an AI clone had been speaking on his behalf for nearly 30 minutes.

So What: This is the clearest template yet for how a smaller bank can leapfrog larger competitors on AI: trade your domain data and workflow access for frontier-lab engineering talent you couldn't hire on your own. The efficiency ratio target (49% to low 40s) is a concrete, trackable claim. Watch whether it lands — it'll set expectations for every bank board asking "what's the ROI?"

OpenAI Restructures Microsoft Exclusivity; AWS in $10B Talks

OpenAI has restructured its exclusive partnership with Microsoft, freeing it to distribute products across rival cloud providers. Amazon is reportedly in talks to invest $10B in OpenAI and use its own AI chips to host OpenAI's models. Meanwhile, Meta signed a multi-billion dollar deal to rent Google's AI chips, driven by a global memory shortage that has pushed Meta's 2026 AI capex guidance to $125–145B.

So What: The cloud layer beneath FS AI deployments is becoming multi-vendor by default. If your firm's AI strategy assumed a single-cloud, single-model architecture, that assumption is being undercut by the labs themselves. Procurement and vendor risk teams should update third-party dependency mappings accordingly.

06

Research & Talent Moves

Cambridge 2026 Report: 81% Adoption, But Transformation Gap Widens

The Cambridge Centre for Alternative Finance released its landmark 2026 Global AI in Financial Services Report, surveying financial institutions, AI vendors, and 130 regulators worldwide. Key findings: 81% of FS firms are adopting AI at some level; 40% report advanced adoption. Fintechs lead incumbents by 47% to 30% in advanced AI use. GenAI and agentic AI are now more widely deployed than traditional ML methods. But only 14% of firms view AI as truly transformational to strategy — and 55% find it difficult to measure AI's value. OpenAI is the most-used foundation model at 76% of industry respondents, followed by Google at 57% and Anthropic at 35%.

So What: The 81% headline masks a sobering reality: most firms are stuck optimising back-office tasks, not reimagining business models. The 14% "transformational" figure is the one to watch. If your firm is in the 86% majority, the competitive risk isn't that you're not using AI — it's that you're using it for the wrong things. The report also confirms a clear fintech advantage, reinforcing the case for incumbent-fintech partnerships.

KPMG: AI Delivering Biggest Gains in Judgment, Not Automation

KPMG's 2026 Global AI in Finance report, based on a survey of 1,013 senior finance leaders, found that AI is producing its strongest performance gains in judgment-heavy work rather than transactional automation. Decision-making quality (70%), decision-making speed (71%), and forecasting accuracy (64%) led the gains. Organisations deploying agentic AI outperformed others by 32 percentage points on average. However, the report also found that 89% of FS tech leaders identify as "innovators or fast followers," while simultaneously struggling with technical debt and talent shortages.

So What: This reframes the AI investment case. The highest-value AI applications aren't in back-office efficiency — they're in improving the quality of human judgment in areas like credit decisions, forecasting, and risk assessment. That's a different business case from cost-cutting, and it demands different sponsorship (front-office, not just COO).

Gartner: Strategic AI to Unlock 10 Margin Points; CFOs Raising Tech Budgets

Gartner predicts that CFOs who implement strategic AI and technology portfolio deployment will unlock an additional 10 points of margin growth by 2029. Three-quarters of CFOs are raising their tech budgets for 2026, with nearly half increasing by 10% or more. Separately, Gartner's 2026 strategic predictions warn that "death by AI" legal claims will exceed 2,000 by year-end due to insufficient risk guardrails, and that by 2028, 90% of B2B buying will be AI-agent intermediated — pushing over $15 trillion through autonomous machine-to-machine exchanges. Meanwhile, embedded AI in cloud ERP is predicted to drive a 30% faster financial close by 2028.

So What: The 10-margin-point prediction gives CFOs the business case they've been waiting for — but it's conditional on "strategic portfolio deployment," not scattered pilots. The legal claims warning is the flip side: firms deploying AI without governance face real litigation exposure. Finance and treasury leaders should treat AI investment as portfolio management — doubling down where governance is mature, pausing where it isn't.

HBR Case Study: BBVA's Bottom-Up AI Adoption — 11,000 Users, 4,800 Custom Tools

Harvard Business Review published a landmark case study (April 2026) on how BBVA scaled generative AI adoption across the bank without top-down mandates. Starting with 3,300 ChatGPT Enterprise licences, employee demand grew organically to 11,000 active users — 83% logging in daily — who created over 4,800 custom internal GPTs and reported saving approximately three hours per week. The bank's approach combined rapid senior leadership training (250 directors including the CEO), a decentralised "champions and wizards" network of 750 internal evangelists, and human-controlled governance where AI never writes directly to central databases without validation. BBVA is now expanding to all 120,000 employees across 25 countries.

So What: This is the most detailed public evidence yet that bottom-up AI adoption beats centralised mandates in banking. The key insight: the demand is already inside your organisation — 90% of professionals are using AI tools, but only 40% of companies have official licences. The gap between those numbers is shadow AI risk. Any FS firm still debating "who gets access" should study BBVA's playbook of structured empowerment with governance guardrails.
